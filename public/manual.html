User Manual

Lash & Brow Anatomy Trainer — every panel and control, top to bottom

Getting started

Open doughboydee.github.io/Artistry-and-Bloom in any modern browser — computer or tablet. Nothing to install, no account, nothing saved anywhere but your own device. The screen has two parts: the control panel on the left and the 3D face on the right. Everything in the panel updates the face instantly.

The control panel scrolls — the sections appear in the same order as this manual. The face responds live to every change.

Moving around the face

  • Turn: click (or touch) and drag on the face.
  • Zoom: scroll wheel, or pinch on a touchscreen.
  • One-click angles: the View section at the bottom of the panel has Front, Profile, and Free orbit buttons. Profile is the working view — that's where lash designs succeed or fail.

Face & Skull — building the client

Ten sliders, each a real measurable feature. Left end → right end:

SliderWhat it changes
Brow bone projectionFlat → heavy. How far the brow ridge juts forward — a heavy ridge is an overhang that tall curls run into.
Eye depthProtruding → deep-set. Deep-set eyes sit back in the socket, changing how much room a curl has.
Upper lid crease heightAbsent/low → high. Where the lid folds.
Lid hoodingNone → heavy. Skin draping over the crease toward the lash line — the single biggest factor in curl choice.
Outer corner tiltDownturned → upturned.
Eye spacingClose-set → wide-set.
Eye opening heightNarrow → tall.
Eye lengthShort → long (inner to outer corner).
Nose base widthNarrow → wide. Moves the nostril landmarks the brow mapping lines are drawn from.
AgeYoung adult (~20) → elderly (~80). The brow descends, the lid drapes, the socket hollows, and the natural lashes and brow tails thin — watch a passing design start to fail.

The Reset button returns the current face to neutral.

Fit test — the safety check

Leave "Test the design against the anatomy" on. Extensions color themselves: red touches the lid, amber is within the safety margin, gray/ghosted is hidden behind the hood from the front, and dark is safe. The per-eye summary below the controls says the same thing in words. The safety margin slider sets how much clearance counts as "close" (0.5 mm default).

Full explanation with diagrams — what red, amber, and ghosted physically mean and how the measurements work — in the fit test deep-dive. Note the colors refresh a moment after you stop moving a slider, and "Left eye" always means the client's left (your screen's right in front view).

Lash design — planning the set

  • Preset maps — one-click classics: natural, cat eye, doll eye, squirrel, open eye, fox eye, kitten, wispy, eyeliner effect. A preset fills the zone table; you can edit from there.
  • Zones (inner → outer) — split the lash line into 3, 5, 7, or 9 zones. Each row sets that zone's Length (8–15 mm), Curl (B, C, CC, D — round curls of increasing lift; L and M — flat-based curls that kick up late, the hooded-eye tools), Diameter (fiber thickness in mm), and Wisp (— / soft / spiky: alternating longer spikes for a textured, wispy look).
  • Natural lashes — the client's own lashes: growth direction, density, curl, thickness, and length. Extensions can only attach where a natural lash grows, so a sparse client gets a sparser set.
  • Natural / Extensions checkboxes — show or hide each layer.
  • Show lash map at the eye — draws the mapping diagram artists make on an under-eye pad, directly on the anatomy: zone boundary ticks and each zone's assignment ("11·C").
  • Precision check — the weight rule: it reads this client's natural lash length, shows the safe maximum (natural + 2 mm), and flags longer zones amber — too heavy for the follicle even when the shape fits.

Brows

  • Sliders — hair density, hair caliber (fine → coarse), growth direction (flat/lateral → upswept), brow position (low → high), and fullness. Brow hair also thins with the Age slider, tail first.
  • Show mapping lines — turns on the brow construction overlay, with a method dropdown:
    • Classic three-point — pencil rays from the nostril edge through the inner corner (green dot = start), pupil (blue = arch), and outer corner (pink = tail).
    • Thread mapping — vertical inked lines for start (above the nostril wing) and arch (outer edge of the iris), plus a horizontal level line: the panel reads out whether each tail ends above or below its start, in mm.
    • Golden ratio — the arch at the phi division of start→tail, with the measured ratio shown against the 1.618 : 1 ideal.
  • Show symmetry guides — horizontal reference lines across both brows plus left-vs-right height differences in mm. Map both sides before trusting your eye.

Move the nose base width or eye sliders with mapping on and watch the dots travel — the lines are recomputed from the landmarks, not painted on.

Comparing two faces

  1. In the View section, check "Compare two faces (same design on both)". The screen splits: Face A left, Face B right.
  2. Use the Face A / Face B tabs at the top of the panel to choose which face the anatomy sliders edit. The lash and brow design is shared — that's the point: one plan, two anatomies, two outcomes.
  3. The fit summaries list both faces. Classic exercise: Face A neutral, Face B hooded and deep-set, same cat-eye map.

Instructor tools

  • Teaching cases — built-in scenarios that load a complete setup (anatomy + design + comparison) in one click.
  • My saved scenarios — type a name and press Save to keep the current setup. Saved scenarios live in this browser on this device; ✕ deletes one.
  • Copy share link — copies a link that contains the entire setup inside the address itself. Send it to anyone; opening it shows exactly what you see. Nothing is uploaded anywhere — the link is the data.
  • Advanced: custom head model — only relevant if you someday commission a 3D artist to sculpt a more realistic head (see MESH_SPEC in the project). The trainer is complete without it; "Return to built-in head" undoes a loaded file.

If something looks wrong

  • Colors haven't updated? The fit verdicts refresh a moment after the sliders rest — give it a beat.
  • Want a clean slate? Reload the page — it always starts neutral. Saved scenarios survive reloads; the working state doesn't, so save anything you want to keep.
  • A shared link looks broken? Damaged links are repaired or ignored automatically; worst case the app offers a "Reset and reload" button that always recovers.
  • Left and right seem swapped? They aren't — sides are always the client's left and right, like every clinical chart.
