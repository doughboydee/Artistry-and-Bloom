Student Guide

Lash & Brow Anatomy Trainer — how the fit test works, and how to read every overlay

The one-minute tour

Set up a client's face with the Face & Skull sliders — every slider is a real, measurable feature (eye depth, lid hooding, crease height, age…). Plan a lash design with the Lash design zone table or a preset map. Then rotate to profile view. That's where designs succeed or fail: from the front almost everything looks fine.

The fit test — what it actually measures

The fit test answers one question: will this design physically work on this face? It doesn't use rules of thumb. Every lash you see on screen is measured, point by point, against the actual 3D skin surface — so when a slider reshapes the lid, the next measurement happens against the new lid. A design can fail in two different ways:

Failure 1 — it touches the skin. Each extension is checked along its whole curve (skipping the first 2 mm at the base, which is glued along the natural lash and is supposed to hug the lid).

Profile view — the view that decides. The dashed line is the safety margin around the skin: an extension crossing it turns amber; one that reaches the skin itself turns red.

Failure 2 — it disappears from the front. A curl that hides behind the hood does nothing for the client. From straight ahead — the direction a person faces you — the test casts a sight-line forward from points along each lash. If skin blocks the view for more than about half of the lash, it is drawn ghosted: faint, see-through gray. It hurts nothing; it's wasted lash.

The occlusion check. The hood's forward overhang blocks the sight-lines to the tucked-under lash, so it renders ghosted — technically applied, visually absent.

Each lash gets one verdict, worst wins:

Red

Touches or presses into the skin. Fix the design.

Ghosted

Hidden from the front. Wasted lash.

Amber

Clears, but inside the safety margin.

Safe

Clear with room to spare.

Reading the summary. "Left eye (screen right): 1 touch the lid (red), 2 within 0.5 mm (amber) — of 51." The 51 is how many extensions were actually placed: extensions only attach where a natural lash grows, so a sparse or aged client has fewer. Left/right always means the client's left/right — the client's left eye is on your screen's right in front view.

The verdict colors arrive a moment after you stop moving a slider — the face and lashes move instantly, and the measurement catches up about a sixth of a second later.

The precision check — a different rule

The fit test is about shape: does the design clear this face? The precision check is about weight: is each extension a safe length for the natural lash that has to carry it? The industry rule is never more than 2–3 mm past the natural lash — longer means follicle strain and early shedding, even when the shape fits perfectly.

The trainer generates the natural lashes, so it checks this rule exactly — and the safe maximum drops as the age slider thins them.

Turn it on with "Precision check" in the Lash design panel: over-length zones are outlined amber in the zone table and flagged ⚠ on the on-lid map. A design can pass the fit test and fail the precision check — a long lash can clear a shallow lid and still be too heavy for a thinned 5 mm natural. Two rules, two lessons.

Three exercises worth doing

  1. D vs L on a hooded lid. Open the teaching case "D vs L on a hooded lid". Watch the D-curl map go red and ghosted where the hood sits, then change the outer zones to L: the flat base slips under the fold and the late lift comes out in front of it.
  2. The same design, aged. Set a doll map on a neutral face, confirm it passes, then slide Age to the right. The brow descends, the lid drapes, and the naturals thin — the same design starts failing both tests. This is why mapping is per-client, not per-style.
  3. Front lies, profile tells. Put any dramatic map on a deep-set, hooded face. Front view: gorgeous. Profile view: read the reds. Make profile the habit.
